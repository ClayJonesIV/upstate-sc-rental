Regional Market Analysis

UPSTATE SC MACRO VIEW

The Upstate South Carolina rental market is showing mixed momentum in June, with landlord-favorable conditions persisting in most markets despite some recent softening. Greenville leads the region at $1,722 average rent with healthy 2.0% monthly growth, while Spartanburg offers the most affordable option at $1,269 with similarly strong 1.9% monthly increases. The higher-end markets like Simpsonville ($2,072) and Piedmont ($1,864) are experiencing balanced conditions, though Piedmont saw modest declines. Anderson maintains steady pricing around $1,409 with minimal monthly movement, while Easley stands out as the only clearly renter-favored market after a 2.2% monthly rent decline to $1,677. Days on market remain reasonable across most markets, ranging from the mid-twenties to mid-forties, suggesting decent absorption despite some markets seeing increased inventory levels.

OUTLOOK AND RISKS

Owners should monitor the diverging trends between strong performers like Greenville and Spartanburg versus markets showing recent weakness like Easley and Piedmont over the next three to six months. The increase in available listings in several markets, particularly Greer and Simpsonville, combined with relatively stable days on market, suggests supply is catching up with demand in some areas. While current conditions remain largely favorable to landlords, the mixed monthly rent performance and rising inventory levels in key markets signal potential moderation ahead. This month, owners should focus on competitive positioning rather than aggressive rent increases, especially in markets like Easley and Piedmont where recent trends suggest tenant leverage is growing. (Sources: RentCast 2026-06-01)

Market Analysis

MARKET CONDITIONS

Greenville's rental market shows balanced conditions with modest landlord advantages taking hold in June. The market delivered a solid 2.0% rent increase to an average of $1,722, while days on market held steady at 33 days after improving from the 46-day mark seen in March. Active listings dropped slightly by 1.6% to 134 units, suggesting demand is absorbing available inventory at a measured pace. The recent trend indicates the market has stabilized after experiencing some loosening earlier in the spring, with rental velocity remaining consistent and pricing power returning to property owners.

IMPLICATIONS FOR CURRENT OWNERS

Current owners should feel cautiously optimistic about market conditions heading into summer. The combination of rising rents and stable absorption times suggests you can maintain confidence in your rental income while avoiding the stress of extended vacancy periods that plagued some markets earlier this year. The slight decline in available inventory indicates competition among tenants is improving, though not dramatically. This environment favors steady, reliable operations over aggressive positioning, making it an ideal time to focus on tenant retention and measured rent optimization rather than taking significant risks with pricing or major capital expenditures.

VACANCY MARKETING STRATEGY

Price new listings at or slightly below the strongest comparable properties in your immediate area to ensure prompt leasing. While days on market have stabilized around 33 days, the improvement from 46 days in March shows the market is still finding its footing. Given the modest inventory decline and steady absorption, you can price confidently at market rates but should avoid pushing above comparable rents unless your property offers clear advantages. The current environment rewards properties that lease quickly over those that test the upper bounds of market pricing, so err toward slightly conservative pricing to minimize vacancy risk and capture the steady demand that's emerging.

RENEWAL PRICING STRATEGY

Approach renewal conversations with modest rent increases that reflect the market's 2.0% monthly improvement while prioritizing tenant retention. The stable days on market and controlled inventory levels support gentle upward pressure on existing rents, but the market hasn't reached a point where aggressive increases are prudent. Focus on renewals that keep good tenants in place with increases that feel reasonable to residents while capturing some of the market's positive momentum. This conservative approach aligns with current market realities and positions you well regardless of how conditions evolve through the remainder of the leasing season. (Sources: RentCast 2026-06-01)

Market Analysis

MARKET CONDITIONS

Spartanburg presents a landlord-favored rental environment with strong underlying fundamentals despite some recent softening in leasing velocity. The market delivered a solid rent increase of 1.9% in June, reaching an average of $1,269, while available inventory dropped sharply by 14.8% to just 96 active listings. However, properties are taking slightly longer to lease, with days on market edging up to 34 days compared to recent months that saw faster absorption. The combination of rising rents and contracting supply suggests robust tenant demand, though the modest uptick in marketing time indicates some price sensitivity among renters as monthly costs climb.

IMPLICATIONS FOR CURRENT OWNERS

Current owners should feel confident about their market position while remaining attentive to tenant retention strategies. The sharp drop in available inventory creates favorable conditions for rent growth, and the market's landlord-favored classification indicates that tenant options remain limited. However, the slight increase in days on market serves as a reminder that pricing discipline matters, particularly as rents continue climbing. Owners with quality properties in desirable locations can expect strong leasing interest, but those with units requiring updates or in secondary locations should focus on competitive positioning rather than aggressive pricing.

VACANCY MARKETING STRATEGY

New vacancy listings should be priced at or slightly below the strongest comparable properties to minimize time on market and capture the current demand while inventory remains tight. The recent uptick in days on market from the low 30s suggests that tenants are becoming more selective, making competitive pricing essential for quick absorption. Given that active listings have dropped significantly while marketing time has only increased modestly, the fundamentals support market-rate pricing for well-positioned properties, but avoid testing the high end of comparable rents. Properties with any condition or location disadvantages should price conservatively below stronger comps to ensure steady leasing velocity in this environment.

RENEWAL PRICING STRATEGY

Current tenants should receive modest rent increases that reflect the market's upward trajectory while prioritizing retention over maximum revenue extraction. The combination of rising market rents and tight inventory supports renewal increases, but the longer marketing times for new listings suggest that aggressive pricing could backfire if tenants choose to move. Property owners should implement increases in the 3-5% range for quality tenants in good standing, allowing them to capture some of the market appreciation while maintaining occupancy stability. Given the current landlord-favored conditions, this conservative approach balances revenue growth with the lower risk and costs associated with tenant retention. (Sources: RentCast 2026-06-01)
