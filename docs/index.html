UPSTATE SC MACRO VIEW

The Upstate South Carolina rental market is showing clear geographic divergence in April 2026, with landlords maintaining pricing power across most submarkets despite mixed monthly performance. IMPLICATIONS FOR CURRENT OWNERS

Current property owners should feel cautiously optimistic but avoid complacency in this environment. This means you cannot simply list at any price and expect quick results like you might have in a red-hot market. The good news is that demand remains reasonably stable with only 50 properties actively competing for tenants, but the longer average marketing time of 51 days means you should be prepared for a more patient leasing process and potentially more tenant negotiations on terms.

VACANCY MARKETING STRATEGY

Given the recent uptick in days on market from 48 to 51 days and the slight increase in active inventory, pricing strategy should favor market rates rather than premium positioning. The three-bedroom average of $1,725 and four-bedroom average of $1,974 provide solid benchmarks, but the 5.1% rent decline suggests pushing above these comps carries meaningful vacancy risk. Properties should be priced at or slightly below comparable units to ensure competitive positioning, especially considering that marketing periods have extended. The conservative approach of pricing 2-3% below stronger comps will likely result in faster leasing and lower overall vacancy costs, which typically outweighs the modest rent premium you might achieve by holding firm at top-market pricing.

RENEWAL PRICING STRATEGY

Renewal decisions should prioritize tenant retention over aggressive rent increases in the current environment. While the market remains slightly landlord-favored, the recent softening in rents and extended vacancy periods make replacing a departing tenant both time-consuming and potentially costly. Modest increases in the 2-4% range may be supportable for strong tenants in desirable properties, but holding rents flat should be the default strategy for average situations. The risk-reward calculation clearly favors keeping reliable tenants in place rather than testing the market with aggressive increases that could trigger turnover into a environment where re-leasing takes nearly two months.
